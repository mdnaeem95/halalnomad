Halal restaurants in Bangkok: 138 places, trust-levelled by travellers

Bangkok is different from most cities on this app in the best way: halal food here isn't an import. Thailand has its own Muslim food culture, and Bangkok has whole neighbourhoods where Thai-Muslim kitchens cook Thai food that was never anything but halal — Ramkhamhaeng especially, further out than the tourist core but the real thing.

The trap is in the middle of the city, where the risk is invisible: pork lard in the wok, oyster sauce in the stir-fry, and street stalls frying everything in the same oil. A chicken dish from a mixed stall isn't the same as a chicken dish from a halal one, even when the ingredients list looks identical. The practical rule: choose dedicated halal stalls and restaurants over asking a mixed kitchen to adapt — and "mai sai nam man moo" (no pork oil) is a phrase worth knowing anyway.

That gap is exactly what the trust levels are for. We never claim a place is "certified" by us — every one of the 138 Bangkok places below carries a community trust level, from Reported to Trusted, built from traveller reports and confirmations. You can see how we know what we know.

Where halal food clusters in Bangkok

One traveller's home base, one local heartland, and the mall belt in between.

Sukhumvit สุขุมวิท

The traveller's halal home base. The cluster around Soi 3 and Nana is the densest — Middle Eastern grills, Thai-Muslim kitchens and South Asian standards within a few minutes' walk — and further down the line Soi 22 and Soi 31 hold reliable sit-down options, so wherever you're staying along the BTS you're rarely more than a couple of stops from a dedicated halal kitchen that doesn't need quizzing about the wok.

Ramkhamhaeng รามคำแหง

Where Bangkok's own halal food culture lives. This is the city's Thai-Muslim heartland — whole streets of kitchens cooking food that was never anything but halal, from khao mok (Thai-style biryani) to southern-Thai curries, at local prices for local people. It sits well outside the tourist core, and our community's coverage out here is still growing — which is exactly the kind of gap traveller reports fill. If you make the trip, add what you find to the app: this is the neighbourhood where every confirmation counts most.

Siam / MBK สยาม

Mid-sightseeing practicality: the malls around Siam solve the halal-lunch problem without a detour. MBK's food floors hold dedicated halal counters and restaurants, and Siam Square's side sois hide a few proper Thai kitchens — so a shopping day doesn't collapse into fruit and fries. Mall food courts are also the easiest place in Bangkok to see what you're getting before you commit.

  • Yana Restaurant · MBK Center
  • Coffee by Farida Ali · MBK, Floor 2
  • Phrigkiao Bangkok · Siam Square Soi 6

How trust levels work

Every place carries a trust level from 1 to 4. Reported: a traveller or public source says there are halal options — start here, and ask. Community-verified: at least three travellers have confirmed it on the ground. Photo-verified: a traveller has uploaded evidence, like a halal-only menu. Trusted: backed by a recognised certification body. We never certify anything ourselves — the levels surface what travellers have actually checked, dish-level caveats included.
