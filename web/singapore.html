Halal restaurants in Singapore: 647 places, 471 Trusted

Counts as of 16 August 2026 — live from our database. The Trusted number moves as certifications are re-checked and lapse.

Singapore's reputation tricks people in the opposite direction from everywhere else on this app. Because the city is famously Muslim-friendly, visitors assume everything is halal — it isn't; most restaurants here aren't. What Singapore actually is, is the easiest place in this part of Asia to know for sure: MUIS, the official certification body, maintains a real register, and certification here means audits and paperwork, not a sticker.

The thing worth learning before your first hawker centre: certification is per-stall, not per-building. One centre can hold certified stalls, Muslim-owned-but-uncertified stalls, and pork noodles, all in one row — so you check the stall, not the centre. And Muslim-owned and MUIS-certified aren't the same claim; plenty of excellent food is the former without the latter. For a first meal, the historic heart of it all is Geylang Serai and Kampong Glam.

This is also the city where our trust system runs at full power: as of the last update, 471 of the 647 Singapore places below are Trusted, backed by MUIS certification records — provenance you can check, that we re-verify because certifications lapse. One curation note: we deliberately leave out the hundreds of certified global fast-food outlets. You don't need an app to find McDonald's; this list is the independents and the institutions.

Where halal food clusters in Singapore

Two historic Malay-Muslim quarters, the Indian-Muslim strip, and the two most useful central areas. Trusted below means MUIS-certified.

Kampong Glam Geylang Serai Bugis Little India Orchard

Kampong Glam

The historic Arab-Malay quarter, and the single densest cluster of halal food on the island — the Sultan Mosque end of Arab Street, Haji Lane and Kandahar Street packed with nasi padang institutions, Middle Eastern grills and cafés. If you eat one meal in Singapore, the odds are best here — and most of it is certified, not just assumed.

  • Hjh Maimunah Trusted · nasi padang, Bib Gourmand
  • Islamic Restaurant Trusted · North Indian, est. 1921
  • The Dim Sum Place Trusted · North Bridge Rd

Geylang Serai

The Malay heartland — less touristed than Kampong Glam and all the better for it. The market and the shophouses around it are where Singaporean-Malay food lives day to day, from ayam penyet to local-chain kitchens that happen to be certified. This is the other historic heart the hero mentions: come hungry, come off-peak.

  • Ayam Penyet President Trusted · Indonesian
  • Sanook Kitchen Trusted · Thai
  • Encik Tan Trusted · local hawker favourites

Bugis

Central, walkable from Kampong Glam, and the easiest place to solve a halal lunch mid-sightseeing without a detour. The stretch toward Arab Street holds long-standing Indian-Muslim and Middle Eastern names — every one below is MUIS-certified.

  • The Landmark Restaurant Trusted · North Indian
  • Tarboush Restaurant Trusted · Arab Street · Middle Eastern
  • Ayam Penyet Ria Trusted · Indonesian

Little India

The Serangoon Road strip is the place for Indian-Muslim food — biryani, dosa, tandoor and late-night supper — and it has one of the highest certified ratios of any area on the island. Almost everything worth queuing for here carries a MUIS record.

  • Indian Saaga Trusted · North Indian
  • Sakunthala's Food Palace Trusted · South Indian
  • Nandhana's Restaurant Trusted · South Indian

Orchard

The shopping belt, and more certified halal than most visitors expect — the mall basements hide certified chains and independents alike, so a day on Orchard Road doesn't collapse into the food-court guessing game. A good place to eat certified while sightseeing.

  • Go-Ang Pratunam Chicken Rice Trusted · Michelin, chicken rice
  • The Dim Sum Place Trusted · The Centrepoint
  • Tambuah Mas Trusted · Indonesian

How trust levels work

Every place carries a trust level from 1 to 4. Reported: a traveller or public source says there are halal options — start here, and ask. Community-verified: at least three travellers have confirmed it on the ground. Photo-verified: a traveller has uploaded evidence, like a halal-only menu. Trusted: backed by a recognised certification body. In Singapore that body is MUIS, and a Trusted badge here is anchored to a real certification record — auditable provenance, which we re-verify because certifications lapse. We never certify anything ourselves, and Muslim-owned is not the same claim as MUIS-certified — the levels surface what has actually been checked, dish-level caveats included.
