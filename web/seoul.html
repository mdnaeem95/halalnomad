Halal restaurants in Seoul: 119 places, trust-levelled by travellers

The tricky thing about eating halal in Seoul isn't finding a halal restaurant — it's that the risk in Korean food is invisible. Soups and ramen are often built on pork-bone broth, and the bowl looks identical whether the base is pork, beef or anchovy. Marinades and sauces frequently carry mirin or cooking wine. So the question that matters isn't "is there pork in it" — it's "what's the broth, and what's in the marinade." Ask that, specifically.

The easy finds cluster in Itaewon, home to the city's densest run of Muslim-owned and certified kitchens — Turkish, Middle Eastern, Malaysian, Pakistani. Halal Korean food is the rarer prize: a small number of dedicated places do halal samgyetang and Korean BBQ, and they're destinations worth planning around, not defaults you'll stumble into.

That gap between "looks fine" and "is fine" is why this database works the way it does. We never claim a place is "certified" by us — every one of the 119 Seoul places below carries a community trust level, from Reported to Trusted, based on what travellers have reported and confirmed. Where halal food clusters in Seoul

Seoul's halal food is concentrated where you'd travel anyway — one dense cluster and two practical pockets.

Itaewon 이태원 Myeongdong 명동 Dongdaemun 동대문

Itaewon 이태원

The densest run of halal kitchens in the city, and the widest spread: Turkish grills next to Malaysian kitchens next to the city's rare dedicated halal Korean tables. If eating well matters to your trip, Itaewon is the anchor — you can walk between a dozen solid options without checking a map, and it's where Seoul's halal Korean BBQ and samgyetang actually live. Start here on night one, find your favourites, then branch out.

  • Makan Halal Restaurant 마칸 할랄 레스토랑 · Malaysian
  • EID Halal Korean Food · Korean
  • Busan Jib Korean BBQ 부산집 할랄 고기 · Korean

Myeongdong 명동

The tourist core — and easier than it looks, once you know which doors to walk through. Between the cosmetics stores and street-food carts there's a working set of halal restaurants doing Korean BBQ, fried chicken and Indian standards, so a shopping afternoon doesn't have to end in a convenience-store dinner. The street-food carts themselves are the usual mixed-kitchen risk — the sit-down halal places are the reliable move here.

  • Namsan Garden Halal Korean BBQ · Korean
  • BSJ Chicken & WOK · Korean
  • Gurkha Indian Restaurant · Indian

Dongdaemun 동대문

Night-market and late-night territory: the markets around the gate run into the small hours, and the halal options here skew hearty — curries and grilled meat rather than dainty portions. Thinner on dedicated halal places than Itaewon, so it rewards picking your restaurant before you're hungry rather than wandering; the two below are the anchors travellers keep coming back to after a night-market run.

  • Halal King · Middle Eastern
  • Everest Curry World · Indian & Nepali

How trust levels work

Every place carries a trust level from 1 to 4. Reported: a traveller or public source says there are halal options — start here, and ask. Community-verified: at least three travellers have confirmed it on the ground. Photo-verified: a traveller has uploaded evidence, like a halal-only menu. Trusted: backed by a recognised certification body. We never certify anything ourselves — the levels surface what travellers have actually checked, dish-level caveats included.
